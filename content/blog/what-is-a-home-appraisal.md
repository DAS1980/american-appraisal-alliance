# What Is a Home Appraisal and Why Does It Matter?

A home appraisal is an independent, professionally supported opinion of a residential property's value as of a specific date. It is prepared by a state-licensed or certified appraiser who inspects or otherwise examines the property, researches the local market, and analyzes sales of comparable homes before reaching a conclusion.

Appraisals matter because many significant financial decisions rely on them: how much a lender will lend, whether a purchase price is supported, how marital property is divided, how an estate is settled, or whether a property tax assessment is reasonable. This article explains what an appraisal is, how the process works, and what homeowners can reasonably expect.

## What a Home Appraisal Is — and What It Is Not

An appraisal is an opinion of value developed under professional standards. In the United States, appraisers follow the **Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP)**, which require that the appraiser be impartial, have no undisclosed interest in the property, and support the conclusions in the report with credible analysis.

In most assignments, the appraiser develops an opinion of **market value** — broadly, the most probable price a property should bring in a competitive and open market, assuming a typically motivated buyer and seller who are both reasonably informed and acting in their own interests. The exact definition used depends on the assignment and the client.

An appraisal is not:

- **A home inspection.** A home inspector evaluates the operation and condition of systems such as the roof, plumbing, and electrical. An appraiser observes condition as it relates to value but does not perform a home inspection.
- **A comparative market analysis (CMA).** A real estate agent's CMA is a pricing estimate prepared to help list or buy a home. It is useful, but it is not prepared under USPAP by a licensed appraiser.
- **A tax assessment.** County appraisal districts value properties in bulk for tax purposes using mass-appraisal methods, which can differ from an individual market value appraisal.

### Who orders the appraisal?

That depends on why the appraisal is needed:

- **Mortgage purchase or refinance:** The lender orders the appraisal, often through an appraisal management company. Appraiser independence rules generally prevent borrowers, agents, and loan officers from selecting or influencing the appraiser.
- **Private purposes:** Homeowners, attorneys, executors, and real estate professionals can engage an appraiser directly — for example, for a [pre-listing appraisal](/pre-listing-appraisal/), a [divorce appraisal](/divorce-appraisal/), an [estate or date-of-death appraisal](/estate-date-of-death-appraisal/), a [tax assessment appeal](/tax-assessment-appeal/), or [PMI removal](/pmi-removal-appraisal/).

## How the Home Appraisal Process Works

While every assignment is different, a typical residential appraisal follows these steps:

1. **Defining the assignment.** The appraiser confirms the client, intended use, type of value, effective date, and scope of work.
2. **Property inspection.** For a full interior appraisal, the appraiser observes the interior and exterior, notes features, condition, and updates, measures the home, and takes photographs. Some assignments use an [exterior-only inspection](/exterior-only-drive-by-appraisal/) or a [desktop appraisal](/desktop-appraisal/) instead.
3. **Market research.** The appraiser studies recent sales, listings, and market trends in the subject property's area.
4. **Analysis.** The appraiser selects comparable sales, makes adjustments for meaningful differences, and applies the valuation approaches that fit the property.
5. **Reconciliation and report.** The appraiser reconciles the evidence into a final opinion of value and documents the support in a written report.

The time required for the inspection and for the finished report varies with the property's size and complexity, the type of report, and current demand. Your appraiser can give you an estimate when the assignment is discussed.

## The Three Approaches to Value

Appraisers consider three recognized approaches and rely on those that are most applicable:

| Approach | How it works | When it carries the most weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sales comparison | Compares the property with similar homes that have sold, adjusting for differences | Most single-family homes in active markets |
| Cost | Estimates the cost to build the improvements new, subtracts depreciation, and adds land value | New construction and unique properties |
| Income | Converts the property's expected rental income into a value indication | Rental and income-producing properties |

For most owner-occupied homes in the Dallas–Fort Worth area, the sales comparison approach is the primary indicator of value. To see how these approaches appear in a finished report, read our guide on [how to read an appraisal report](/blog/how-to-read-an-appraisal-report/).

## What Influences an Appraised Value?

![Appraisal report highlighting the components of a property valuation](/images/blog/what-is-a-home-appraisal/appraisal-report-components.jpg)

Appraised value reflects how the market responds to the property's characteristics, including:

- **Location:** neighborhood, school district, access, and nearby influences
- **Size and layout:** gross living area, bedroom and bathroom count, and functional utility
- **Condition and quality:** maintenance, construction quality, and the age and quality of updates
- **Site features:** lot size, view, and improvements such as pools or outbuildings
- **Market conditions:** supply, demand, and price trends as of the effective date

### Preparing for the inspection

Preparation helps the inspection go smoothly, but it is important to keep expectations accurate. Cleaning and decluttering make it easier for the appraiser to access and observe the home; they do not by themselves raise market value. What does help is information: a list of significant improvements with approximate dates and costs, permits for additions, and notes on any recent repairs. Make sure the appraiser can reach the attic, garage, mechanical areas, and every room.

## Why a Home Appraisal Matters

- **Buyers** gain independent support for the price they are paying.
- **Sellers** can use a [pre-listing appraisal](/pre-listing-appraisal/) to set an informed asking price.
- **Lenders** rely on the appraisal to evaluate the collateral securing a loan.
- **Attorneys, executors, and homeowners** use appraisals for divorce, estate settlement, and tax matters where an impartial, well-supported value is needed.
